Havenmark Compliance Casebook 合规案例集

Worked examples of findings, with the grade that was applied and the reasoning.
These are illustrations of judgement, not a substitute for the grading rules
themselves, which live in the Pathway document.

本文为案例说明，分级规则以进阶体系文件为准。

CASE 01 — Undated grading sheet
An agent's case file contained a client grading sheet with no date of last
change. The grade itself was correct and the client was being handled properly.
Graded MINOR. Corrected within five working days. No standing impact.
Reasoning: the harm is to future reviewability, not to the client.

CASE 02 — Grading four months stale
A different file carried a grade last changed in March, reviewed in July. The
client had bought elsewhere in May. Graded MAJOR. The agent's next three cases
were reviewed.
Reasoning: a stale grade is not a paperwork gap. It means the pipeline the team
leader reviewed described a client who no longer existed.

CASE 03 — Document issued one stage early
A tenancy agreement was sent to a prospective tenant before the good faith
deposit was received. No loss occurred; the tenant proceeded normally.
Graded MAJOR regardless of outcome.
Reasoning: grading follows the act, not the luck. The same act with a different
tenant creates a signed agreement with no deposit behind it.

CASE 04 — Price quoted from memory
An agent quoted a psf figure in a WhatsApp message, drawn from a transaction
they remembered rather than from a lodged caveat. The figure was approximately
correct. Graded CRITICAL. The case was suspended and the Practice Committee
convened the following morning.
Reasoning: approximately correct is the dangerous case, not the safe one. A
wildly wrong figure is questioned; a plausible one is relied upon.

CASE 05 — Undisclosed family interest
An agent marketed a unit owned by a sibling without written disclosure to the
buyer. Graded CRITICAL.
Reasoning: the disclosure obligation exists precisely because the agent
believes the conflict is immaterial. That belief is what it is protecting
against.

CASE 06 — Split changed after offer
A co-broke split was renegotiated from fifty-fifty to sixty-forty after an offer
was on the table. Graded CRITICAL and referred to the Practice Committee, which
restored the default split.
Reasoning: the renegotiation was not agreed before the first viewing, and
leverage after an offer is not a basis for allocating work already done.

CASE 07 — Missing meter photographs at handover
A handover inventory was signed by both parties without photographs of the meter
readings. A billing dispute followed six weeks later and could not be resolved
from the file. Graded MINOR at the time, escalated to MAJOR when the dispute
arose.
Reasoning: a finding is graded on the exposure it creates, and the exposure was
only visible once the dispute existed.

CASE 08 — AI-drafted post published without review
A market post generated with AI assistance went out without a named reviewer.
It cited a figure that could not be traced to a caveat. Taken down within one
working day. Graded MAJOR.
Reasoning: the tool cannot be answerable for a claim. The absence of a named
reviewer is the finding, independent of whether the number was right.

CASE 09 — Correct escalation, no finding
An agent was asked by a client to quote a price before a caveat check was
possible. The agent declined and escalated the same day. NO FINDING recorded.
Reasoning: the casebook records correct handling as well as failures. An agent
reading only failures learns what to hide rather than what to do.

CASE 10 — Handover completed with keys uncounted
Access cards were handed over without the count being recorded. Graded MINOR.
Reasoning: keys are the one handover category with no photograph requirement, so
the count is the only record that exists. Missing it leaves nothing at all.

HOW FINDINGS ARE USED

Findings attach to the case, not to the agent's character. An agent with three
Minor findings and clean escalation behaviour is in a better position than one
with none and a habit of not raising things.

合规记录归属于案件，不是对顾问的评价。
